# Dairy desserts · formulation within format

**Closed by Julia on 16 November 2026.** Of 4,261 assignments, names and OFF tags identified a commercial format for 2,725 (64.0%). For 1,355 the format was unspecified, 110 had conflicting cues and 71 raised a comparability concern. Retain these 1,536 assignments in the broader dairy-dessert population under the existing measurement rules. Their disposition is final for this follow-up. Format-specific conclusions apply to the identifiable subset, whose representativeness is unknown.

**Learning for the Radar.** Establish format from independent commercial evidence: a product name, a specific OFF tag or corroborating wording. Nutrient values can prompt a check, but cannot supply the format label. The effect of changing the protein denominator depends on the peer group. In France, the share moving at least 25 percentile points when ranked per calorie was 55.2% for mousse, 47.0% for flan, 23.7% for dessert cream/pudding and 19.9% for tiramisu. Each percentage uses ranks within its own form.

**Interpretation of protein pudding.** The measured profile below may coincide with an established commercial proposition. Record the commercial subformat, measured formulation and assessed pack communication separately. The 2.7% French and 41.7% UK/Irish figures describe the captured cream/pudding populations with their different naming mixes. Market prevalence and a natural cluster boundary remain undetermined.

**Decision. Retain one candidate formulation profile within dessert creams and puddings. For mousse and flan, report the protein–energy trade-off within the commercial format. The other forms have insufficient evidence for a further split.**

**Candidate definition.** For a comparable dessert cream or pudding, require at least 8 g measured protein and at most 110 kcal per 100 g, with valid measurements and the existing 10 kcal analytical floor. These are exploratory cutoffs chosen for this follow-up. A product can qualify on its own measurements. Neither the product name's protein wording nor its claims enter the nutritional rule.

**Why keep this profile.** The qualifying products have closely matching nutritional medians in both primary regions, including two measurements outside the definition, fat and sugars. Among the other measurable products, median protein was 3.1 g in France and 3.4 g in UK/Ireland, and median sugars were 15.0 and 15.4 g respectively. Tightening the rule to ≥9 g protein and ≤100 kcal retained all 27 and 15. Widening it to ≥7 g and ≤120 kcal added two French products. Removing barcodes also seen in the other primary region retained 23 French and 11 UK/Irish qualifiers. This supports a recurring product profile. An optimal dividing line and regional assortment prevalence remain unestablished.

**Commercial wording matters.** France includes 629 measurable products explicitly named crème dessert, compared with just three in UK/Ireland. Within products explicitly named pudding there were 7 qualifiers among 28 in France and 12 among 13 in UK/Ireland. Replication concerns the composition of identifiable products. The large difference in observed shares also reflects the mix captured by OFF and by the wording screen.

**Why rankings change.** Within French mousse alone, products that rose ≥25 percentile points on protein per calorie had median protein of 4.6 g, energy of 151 kcal and fat of 5.7 g. Those that fell by the same margin had *more* protein per 100 g (7.1 g), alongside 348 kcal and 25.0 g fat. Median sugars differed less (18 versus 21 g). UK/Irish mousse showed the same direction with six rising and five falling products. In French flans, rising and falling products had median fat of 0.8 versus 7.4 g and energy of 95 versus 204 kcal. The denominator mathematically determines the re-ranking; the measured fat differences explain what distinguishes these foods in practice. They cannot establish recipe changes.

**Where to stop.** French mousse had 504 measurable products and just one qualifier. Four of 31 UK/Irish mousses qualified. None of the 247 measurable French flans qualified, although 27 of 71 upper-protein flans also fell in the upper energy quartile with protein density at or below the median. These formats demonstrate variation with fat and energy, with insufficient evidence for another reproducible segment. Regional cells below 30 remain descriptive. Crème brûlée, tiramisu and other named forms stay separate.

**Examples and the boundary.** French High-Protein-Pudding – Vanilla (10.0 g protein, 76 kcal) and UK/Irish White Chocolate Pudding (10.0 g, 80.8 kcal) sit near the centres of their respective qualifying groups. French Crème dessert protéinée chocolat has the same 10.0 g protein but 112 kcal, just outside the energy cutoff. UK/Irish Light choc pots has 95 kcal but only 4.6 g protein. These examples show why both measurements matter and why a product close to the cutoff deserves its actual values alongside the label. GTINs and additional examples appear below.

**Population and methods.** The [versioned analysis](dairy_dessert_formulations_v1.py) reuses the shared Wave 2 reader, nutritional validity rules, assignment keys and provisional dessert classification. It reproduces the original H3 rank-change proportions on 3,568 FR, 250 UK/IE and 46 US/CA assignments before narrowing the peers. Commercial subforms use normalized names and tags without nutrition or claim fields. Unspecified and conflicting evidence stays unresolved. Powder, frozen, sauce and infant-food wording triggers a scope caution. Subforms are analytical interpretations of available text. Within each form and region, ranks use average ties on identical measurable products. Nutrient summaries use g/100 g and kcal/100 g, except the explicit g protein/100 kcal column. Counts include repeated GTINs across regions unless the shared-barcode sensitivity says otherwise. All proportions describe the measured OFF population.
