# Biscuits and wafers · formulation CHECK

**Closed following Julia’s review, 16 November 2026.** Retain biscuits and wafers as commercial formats. Report variation within biscuits; evidence for a repeatable wafer split remains insufficient. The illustrative percentile screens are recorded for audit only.

**Biscuits — retain the commercial format and describe nutritional variation.** Even at similar sugars, median absolute differences in fat, salt and protein were 5.00, 0.30 and 1.20 g/100 g across 7,210 French pairs and 5.40, 0.25 and 1.20 across 781 UK/Irish pairs. Tightening the sugar tolerance gave similar results. Among explicitly coated biscuits, the median fat gap was smaller in both regions (2.00 and 2.65 g). Wording about construction explains part of what a broad biscuit comparison captures.

The group 2 versus group 3 contrast survives within biscuits: at similar sugars and matched construction wording, group 2 had median paired differences of **+6.00 g fat, +0.31 g salt and −1.70 g protein** in France, and **+7.60, +0.22 and −1.70 g** in UK/Ireland. Thus some of the original pooled distinction occurs inside this format. The model used those same nutrients to assign groups. Its contrast describes the selected groups; its size cannot independently validate a segment or quantify the causal contribution of product mix.

**Independent product check.** Ignoring group labels, take biscuit pairs with at least 5 g difference in fat. In both primary regions only 18.5% also had at least 0.10 g more salt and 0.50 g less protein in the higher-fat biscuit. The opposite salt/protein direction was nearly as common (638 versus 708 pairs in France; 63 versus 77 in UK/Ireland). Among explicitly named cookies the ordering even reversed in UK/Ireland. The data support variation on multiple nutrient dimensions, with limited evidence for a distinct recurring three-nutrient segment.

A rule applicable to one biscuit (fat ≥23.6 g, salt ≥0.67 g and protein ≤6.0 g per 100 g) captures 814/14,434 measurable French products and 157/1,571 UK/Irish products. A French Belgian chocolate chunk cookie qualifies at 26.0, 0.90 and 5.4 g. A UK/Irish Double Trouble Chocolate Cookie falls just outside at 23.5, 0.80 and 5.7 g. Small changes in the chosen percentiles substantially alter coverage. Keep this as an illustrative screen; the evidence gives no defensible boundary for a named biscuit segment.

**Wafers — retain the format; close the proposed split as exploratory.** Among all sugar-matched wafers the median fat gaps were 4.25 g in France and 3.95 g in UK/Ireland. The salt gaps were 0.12 and 0.14 g. The French control comparison yielded 55 cross-group pairs. Only one UK/Irish wafer was assigned to group 3 and none could be paired under the matching rules. Independently of the control, the proposed three-nutrient direction occurred in just 10/205 French and 1/14 UK/Irish pairs with a fat gap of at least 5 g. A wafer-specific product screen captured 15/872 French and 1/81 UK/Irish products. This supports reporting differences between individual wafers, with insufficient evidence for a repeatable subtype.

**Scope and interpretation.** The starting denominators reconciled to 18,083 FR, 1,863 UK/IE and 10,749 US/CA named biscuit/wafer assignments in the approved inventory. Protein, total fat, total sugars and salt were all valid in 16,425, 1,691 and 1,018 respectively. US/CA results describe a selected subset. The analysis keeps the corrected commercial assignments and derives provisional finer labels from names and OFF tags. A specific wafer term can refine a generic biscuit tag. Wafer wording conflicting with a specific biscuit style stays unresolved. Combined umbrella tags alone are insufficient. Possible crackers, macarons, pastries and other noncomparable forms remain in the coverage table. Filling and coating require explicit wording; “unspecified” permits either to be present.

**Reading the evidence.** F/S/P means fat, salt and protein in that order, all in g/100 g. Within each region, form and construction cue, pair adjacent products by sugars with a maximum gap of 2 g/100 g and no reuse. The sensitivity uses 1 g and a different seeded ordering for sugar ties. Absolute-gap columns give the median for each nutrient separately. Signed control differences use one group 2 and one group 3 product per pair. The independent co-occurrence check uses the direction and magnitudes above; rows for named styles are separate restricted comparisons. The magnitude cutoffs and product screens are exploratory analytical choices. Product screens use French within-form 65th percentiles for fat and salt and the 35th for protein, transferred unchanged to the other regions. The 60/40 and 70/30 columns show sensitivity. Coverage uses valid core-four products in that form. Names, tags and nutrition come from the corrected master and approved baseline. [Versioned analysis](biscuits_and_wafers_v1.py)
